
The veins pronounce themselves 
in the typist's hands, ballooning
like the blueberries in Ipswitch, MA
in August and September. 
Stretching the fabric, like boys 
wearing tight shirts
to feel like men, because 
a man is matter,
a taking of space,
a potential for violence.
The typist scribes 
words of 
thinkers or of 
sayers, 
and what is the difference?

An endless posturing,
a preening,
a relentless display of the self.  
Vanity is the only pastime; we go 
toward the world with razors bared,
flocking like peacocks,
like F-16s.
