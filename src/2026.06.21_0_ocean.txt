
I'm getting over my fear 
of the ocean (I dunk my head
and recognize the threads 
pulled tightly between me
and every whale, every joy,
every thrashing bluefin tuna. 
A million taught reminders
that the only separation 
between anything, really,
is faulty eyesight and the gloom). 

I'm getting over my fear of 
the ocean (dunk your head
and open your eyes, see
the haze, silt,
sun rays,
the funneling, fast,
of all somber things toward you). 

I'm getting over 
my fear of the ocean,
dark water and sea weed. 
Water (dark or light) is
the thing of life and it washes 
over me. Warmer than liquor,
hard heavy chains. 

I'm getting over my
fear 
of the ocean, the red kelp
that swills in the shallow parts,
churned up by some cyclic feud
between sisters, the red kelp
that you must slip through
to get to the clear. 

I'm getting over my fear of the ocean,
and other fears rush in
like the surf, or like washing the dishes,
and you submerge a bowl 
into the washbasin. 
The basin resists 
the submersion, 
protests
against the foreign, 
but the lip of the bowl 
dips below the waterline, 
and the water knows there's nothing to fear. 
Just a new home. 

Like a hermit crab or
a hermit, 
water forms no attachments,
heeds no call. 
Playing a wordless (rhymeless,
timeless) game called gravity.
