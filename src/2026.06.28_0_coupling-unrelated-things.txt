
Lights are on
in the middle story of the Victorian
across the street. 
They came on as the sun sank, or
they were on the whole time.

I tend to couple unrelated things.
The darkening edges bring down
a taste of charcoal, 
as if there was burning 
way up there, a bonfire
with all my friends 
and they were laughing
and drinking up all my whiskey, 
which doesn't bother me much at all, 
because it's bad whiskey and if I try
I can hear them
singing and they suck. 
Singing out the window
of a sinking ship.
Smiling; going down with it. 
Full-bodied, bawdy, still full
from this morning's meusli,
which was cold with black
berries. Buried in a cloudbank
are the coals,
are the mountain tops.
The same cloudbank, golden
still, that loses itself
so as to keep the ground green. 

- The foals in the fields grow stronger by the day.
- We (fools) move along our obtuse angles.
